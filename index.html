About

My interest in intelligence started in cognitive psychology and neuroscience. The work of Francisco Varela on embodied cognition was a particular influence, and these are ideas I find myself returning to now that I work on robotic systems. Switching to computer engineering was less a change of direction than a shift in method, since I wanted to study the same questions from a quantitative angle.

I became interested in computer vision through remote sensing, applying deep learning to satellite imagery for deforestation detection and biodiversity monitoring. In parallel, I also invested my spare time as an undergrad studying reinforcement learning, where we organized a study group with professor Rodrigo Toro to study Sutton and Barto's reinforcement learning book. I was fortunate to be able to combine both interests in UAV-based agricultural surveillance at the National Institute of Informatics in Tokyo, work that led to a publication at ICRA 2025.

I later became focused in large scale data processing and training in LLMs in project LatamGPT at CENIA, Latin America's largest open multilingual LLM. This is a nationally prominent initiative, which allowed me to gain valuable hands-on experience with large-scale distributed pre-training. My current focus is back on research, specifically in world models for robotic control. Our team is currently working on diffusion-based policies for behavioural cloning and JEPA architectures with action encoding for planning, where we hope provide robotic systems with adaptative yet efficient goal reaching behavior.
